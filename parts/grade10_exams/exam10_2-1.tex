\subsection*{Assessment and evaluation}\label{sec:grade10-exam2-1-evaluation}

This exam evaluates only the criteria listed below and uses this order consistently:
\begin{enumerate}
	\item C1 Mean and standard deviation in sample and population.
	\item C2 Population parameter vs sample statistic (estimation of the parameter).
	\item C3 Point estimation vs interval estimation.
	\item C5 Confidence interval for the mean with variance known.
	\item C4 Interpretation in economy/finance context.
\end{enumerate}
Each criterion is evaluated across the three points below.
A student passes a criterion if it is correctly activated in at least two of the three points.
The overall exam result is binary (Passed/Not passed) and is determined by the set of criteria passed.

\begin{enumerate}
	\item A school cafeteria tracks daily net profit during a two-week festival to estimate the population mean profit per day.
	The cafeteria has historical records indicating that the population standard deviation of daily profit is $15$ (in hundreds of dollars),
	and the population mean is unknown. In the most recent festival, a random sample of $36$ days produced a sample mean daily profit
	of $52$ (in hundreds of dollars) and a sample standard deviation of $12$ (in hundreds of dollars). The administration wants a
	$95\%$ confidence interval for the population mean daily profit to guide future pricing decisions.

	\begin{itemize}
		\item Identify the sample mean and sample standard deviation, and state the population mean and population standard deviation, explaining which are statistics and which are parameters.
		\item Explain how the sample statistics are used to estimate the population mean in this setting.
		\item State the point estimate for the population mean and describe how it differs from an interval estimate.
		\item Construct the $95\%$ confidence interval for the population mean assuming the population variance is known.
		\item Interpret the confidence interval for daily profit in the cafeteria's pricing context.
	\end{itemize}
	\item A delivery cooperative wants to estimate the population mean fuel cost per delivery (in dollars) for budgeting.
	Audit data from prior years indicate the population standard deviation of fuel cost per delivery is $5.0$ dollars, while the
	population mean is unknown. A random sample of $50$ deliveries in the current season produced a sample mean fuel cost of
	$18.4$ dollars and a sample standard deviation of $4.2$ dollars. The cooperative requests a $90\%$ confidence interval for the
	population mean fuel cost to guide pricing of delivery contracts.

	\begin{itemize}
		\item Identify the sample mean and sample standard deviation, and state the population mean and population standard deviation, explaining which are statistics and which are parameters.
		\item Explain how the sample statistics estimate the population mean in this budgeting context.
		\item State the point estimate for the population mean and contrast it with an interval estimate.
		\item Construct the $90\%$ confidence interval for the population mean assuming the population variance is known.
		\item Interpret the confidence interval for fuel cost per delivery in an economic decision-making context.
	\end{itemize}
	\item A community sports center studies annual membership spending per member to plan next year's budget.
	From long-term accounting data, the population standard deviation of annual spending per member is known to be $12$ dollars,
	while the population mean is unknown. A random sample of $40$ members reports an average annual spending of $52$ dollars per member,
	with a sample standard deviation of $10$ dollars. Management wants a $99\%$ confidence interval for the population mean annual
	spending per member to evaluate financial sustainability.

	\begin{itemize}
		\item Identify the sample mean and sample standard deviation, and state the population mean and population standard deviation, explaining which are statistics and which are parameters.
		\item Explain how the sample statistics estimate the population mean in this membership-spending context.
		\item State the point estimate for the population mean and distinguish it from an interval estimate.
		\item Construct the $99\%$ confidence interval for the population mean assuming the population variance is known.
		\item Interpret the confidence interval in terms of the sports center's financial planning decisions.
	\end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}
