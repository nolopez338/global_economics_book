		\item \textbf{Structured solution}

	\subsection*{Problem description}
	A school cafeteria must choose one of three menu plans for a two-week festival: Plan A (local produce focus),
	Plan B (mixed menu), or Plan C (bulk staples). Demand for meals can be high, medium, or low. Based on past festivals, the probability of high demand is $0.35$, medium demand is $0.45$, and low demand is $0.20$. Profits are measured in hundreds of dollars. If demand is high, Plan A yields 92, Plan B yields 70, and Plan C yields 55. If demand is medium, Plan A yields 50, Plan B yields 62, and Plan C yields 48. If demand is low, Plan A yields $-8$, Plan B yields 30, and Plan C yields 38. The stated probabilities must be used for an expected value comparison as part of the decision analysis.
	
	\paragraph{Interpreting decision alternatives, events, consequences, and states of nature}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-1-c2}
	
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.36\linewidth}Y@{}}
			\toprule
			Element & Description \\
			\midrule
			Decision alternatives & Plan A (local produce), Plan B (mixed menu), Plan C (bulk staples) \\
			States of nature & High demand (0.35), Medium demand (0.45), Low demand (0.20) \\
			Events & Demand level observed after selecting a plan \\
			Consequences & Profit (hundreds of dollars) for each plan--demand pair \\
			Probabilities & 0.35, 0.45, 0.20 for high, medium, low demand \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Building the payoff table from the description}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-1-c3}
	
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.28\linewidth}C C C C@{}}
			\toprule
			State of nature & Probability & Plan A & Plan B & Plan C \\
			\midrule
			High demand & 0.35 & 92 & 70 & 55 \\
			Medium demand & 0.45 & 50 & 62 & 48 \\
			Low demand & 0.20 & -8 & 30 & 38 \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximax criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-1-c4}
	
	We ignore probabilities and select the plan with the highest possible payoff.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan A}, S_i) &= 92 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan B}, S_i) &= 70 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan C}, S_i) &= 55
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{92, 70, 55\} = 92
	\]
	The Maximax rule recommends Plan A.
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximin criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-1-c5}
	
	We focus on the worst payoff for each plan.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan A}, S_i) &= -8 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan B}, S_i) &= 30 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(\text{Plan C}, S_i) &= 38
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{-8, 30, 38\} = 38
	\]
	The Maximin rule recommends Plan C.
	
	\paragraph{Formulating the decision problem for an optimal choice}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-1-c1}
	
	We compute expected profits using the stated probabilities.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	EV_A &= 0.35(92) + 0.45(50) + 0.20(-8) \\
	&= 32.2 + 22.5 - 1.6 = 53.1 \\
	EV_B &= 0.35(70) + 0.45(62) + 0.20(30) \\
	&= 24.5 + 27.9 + 6 = 58.4 \\
	EV_C &= 0.35(55) + 0.45(48) + 0.20(38) \\
	&= 19.25 + 21.6 + 7.6 = 48.45
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{53.1, 58.4, 48.45\} = 58.4
	\]
	The expected value criterion recommends Plan B.
	
		\item \textbf{Structured solution}

	\subsection*{Problem description}
	A delivery cooperative is choosing a fleet strategy for the next season: Strategy A (electric vans),
	Strategy B (hybrid vans), or Strategy C (diesel vans). Demand for deliveries can be strong, steady, or weak, and fuel costs can be low or high. The cooperative estimates the demand probabilities as $0.30$ (strong), $0.50$ (steady), and $0.20$ (weak). Fuel costs are independent of demand, with probabilities $0.55$ (low) and $0.45$ (high). Revenue, in thousands of dollars, depends on the demand level and strategy. Under strong demand, revenue is 420 for Strategy A, 380 for Strategy B, and 340 for Strategy C. Under steady demand, revenue is 320 for Strategy A, 300 for Strategy B, and 270 for Strategy C. Under weak demand, revenue is 210 for Strategy A, 230 for Strategy B, and 220 for Strategy C. Operating costs, in thousands of dollars, depend on the fuel cost level and strategy. If fuel costs are low, operating costs are 160 for Strategy A, 190 for Strategy B, and 210 for Strategy C. If fuel costs are high, operating costs are 220 for Strategy A, 250 for Strategy B, and 290 for Strategy C. The cooperative should treat each combined demand and fuel-cost outcome as a state of nature and use the stated probabilities to compute expected profits.
	
	\paragraph{Interpreting decision alternatives, events, consequences, and states of nature}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-2-c2}
	
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.36\linewidth}Y@{}}
			\toprule
			Element & Description \\
			\midrule
			Decision alternatives & Strategy A (electric), Strategy B (hybrid), Strategy C (diesel) \\
			States of nature & Combined demand and fuel-cost outcomes (6 states) \\
			Events & Demand level and fuel cost realized after the strategy choice \\
			Consequences & Profit (thousands of dollars) for each strategy--state pair \\
			Probabilities & Demand: 0.30, 0.50, 0.20; Fuel cost: 0.55, 0.45; independent \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Building the payoff table from revenue and cost data}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-2-c3}
	
	Combined state probabilities are obtained by multiplying demand and fuel-cost probabilities.
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.28\linewidth}C C C C@{}}
			\toprule
			State of nature & Probability & Strategy A & Strategy B & Strategy C \\
			\midrule
			Strong demand, low cost & 0.165 & 260 & 190 & 130 \\
			Strong demand, high cost & 0.135 & 200 & 130 & 50 \\
			Steady demand, low cost & 0.275 & 160 & 110 & 60 \\
			Steady demand, high cost & 0.225 & 100 & 50 & -20 \\
			Weak demand, low cost & 0.110 & 50 & 40 & 10 \\
			Weak demand, high cost & 0.090 & -10 & -20 & -70 \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximax criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-2-c4}
	
	We select the largest possible profit for each strategy.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(A, S_i) &= 260 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(B, S_i) &= 190 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(C, S_i) &= 130
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{260, 190, 130\} = 260
	\]
	The Maximax rule recommends Strategy A.
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximin criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-2-c5}
	
	We compare the worst outcomes across strategies.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(A, S_i) &= -10 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(B, S_i) &= -20 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(C, S_i) &= -70
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{-10, -20, -70\} = -10
	\]
	The Maximin rule recommends Strategy A.
	
	\paragraph{Formulating the decision problem for an optimal choice}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-2-c1}
	
	Expected profits are computed with the combined-state probabilities.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	EV_A &= 0.165(260) + 0.135(200) + 0.275(160) + 0.225(100) + 0.110(50) + 0.090(-10) \\
	&= 42.9 + 27 + 44 + 22.5 + 5.5 - 0.9 = 141.0 \\
	EV_B &= 0.165(190) + 0.135(130) + 0.275(110) + 0.225(50) + 0.110(40) + 0.090(-20) \\
	&= 31.35 + 17.55 + 30.25 + 11.25 + 4.4 - 1.8 = 93.0 \\
	EV_C &= 0.165(130) + 0.135(50) + 0.275(60) + 0.225(-20) + 0.110(10) + 0.090(-70) \\
	&= 21.45 + 6.75 + 16.5 - 4.5 + 1.1 - 6.3 = 35.0
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{141.0, 93.0, 35.0\} = 141.0
	\]
	The expected value criterion recommends Strategy A.
	
		\item \textbf{Structured solution}

	\subsection*{Problem description}
	A community sports center must choose a membership model for the next year: Model A (standard monthly pass),
	Model B (premium pass with classes), or Model C (flex pass). Demand for memberships can be high, medium, or low, and staffing costs can be favorable or unfavorable. Demand probabilities are $0.25$ (high), $0.50$ (medium), and $0.25$ (low). Staffing costs are independent of demand, with probabilities $0.60$ (favorable) and $0.40$ (unfavorable). Expected membership counts depend on demand and model: under high demand the center expects 620 members with Model A, 480 with Model B, and 700 with Model C; under medium demand it expects 460 with Model A, 360 with Model B, and 520 with Model C; under low demand it expects 300 with Model A, 240 with Model B, and 360 with Model C. Membership fees are $35$ for Model A, $48$ for Model B, and $30$ for Model C per member per year. Variable staffing cost per member is $18$ when costs are favorable and $26$ when costs are unfavorable. Fixed annual costs are $6{,}500$ for Model A, $9{,}000$ for Model B, and $5{,}200$ for Model C. All monetary amounts in this point can be treated in dollars. Use the combined demand and staffing outcomes as states of nature and the stated probabilities to compute expected profits.
	
	\paragraph{Interpreting decision alternatives, events, consequences, and states of nature}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-3-c2}
	
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.36\linewidth}Y@{}}
			\toprule
			Element & Description \\
			\midrule
			Decision alternatives & Model A (standard), Model B (premium), Model C (flex) \\
			States of nature & Combined demand and staffing-cost outcomes (6 states) \\
			Events & Demand and staffing-cost conditions observed after selection \\
			Consequences & Profit (dollars) for each model--state pair \\
			Probabilities & Demand: 0.25, 0.50, 0.25; Staffing: 0.60, 0.40; independent \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Building the payoff table from the narrative data}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-3-c3}
	
	Using profit = (members $\times$ fee) $-$ (members $\times$ variable cost) $-$ fixed cost.
	\begin{center}
		\begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{@{}L{0.30\linewidth}C C C C@{}}
			\toprule
			State of nature & Probability & Model A & Model B & Model C \\
			\midrule
			High demand, favorable cost & 0.15 & 4040 & 5400 & 3200 \\
			High demand, unfavorable cost & 0.10 & -920 & 1560 & -2400 \\
			Medium demand, favorable cost & 0.30 & 1320 & 1800 & 1040 \\
			Medium demand, unfavorable cost & 0.20 & -2360 & -1080 & -3120 \\
			Low demand, favorable cost & 0.15 & -1400 & -1800 & -880 \\
			Low demand, unfavorable cost & 0.10 & -3800 & -3720 & -3760 \\
			\bottomrule
		\end{tabularx}
	\end{center}
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximax criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-3-c4}
	
	We compare the best-case profits for each model.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(A, S_i) &= 4040 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(B, S_i) &= 5400 \\
	\max_i \text{Payoff}(C, S_i) &= 3200
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{4040, 5400, 3200\} = 5400
	\]
	The Maximax rule recommends Model B.
	
	\paragraph{Applying the Maximin criterion}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-3-c5}
	
	We compare the worst outcomes for each model.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(A, S_i) &= -3800 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(B, S_i) &= -3720 \\
	\min_i \text{Payoff}(C, S_i) &= -3760
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{-3800, -3720, -3760\} = -3720
	\]
	The Maximin rule recommends Model B.
	
	\paragraph{Formulating the decision problem for an optimal choice}\label{subsubsec:grade10-exam2-1-solution-3-c1}
	
	Expected profits use the combined-state probabilities.
	\[
	\begin{aligned}
	EV_A &= 0.15(4040) + 0.10(-920) + 0.30(1320) + 0.20(-2360) + 0.15(-1400) + 0.10(-3800) \\
	&= 606 - 92 + 396 - 472 - 210 - 380 = -152 \\
	EV_B &= 0.15(5400) + 0.10(1560) + 0.30(1800) + 0.20(-1080) + 0.15(-1800) + 0.10(-3720) \\
	&= 810 + 156 + 540 - 216 - 270 - 372 = 648 \\
	EV_C &= 0.15(3200) + 0.10(-2400) + 0.30(1040) + 0.20(-3120) + 0.15(-880) + 0.10(-3760) \\
	&= 480 - 240 + 312 - 624 - 132 - 376 = -580
	\end{aligned}
	\]
	\[
	\max\{-152, 648, -580\} = 648
	\]
	The expected value criterion recommends Model B.
