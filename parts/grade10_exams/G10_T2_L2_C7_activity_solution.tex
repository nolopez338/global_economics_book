		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A city lab considers a pilot program for a new testing service over the next budget cycle. The net profit from the program depends on two demand conditions, where $S_1$ represents strong demand in that period and $S_2$ represents weak demand in that period. The table entries 15 and -5 are the lab's net profit in the same monetary units used by the city budget for the pilot under $S_1$ and $S_2$.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lcc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p) & S_2\,(1-p) \\
			\hline
			\text{Pilot} & 15 & -5 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p$ be the lab's assessed probability that strong demand $S_1$ occurs during the pilot period, so $1-p$ is the probability of weak demand $S_2$ in the same period. Determine for which values of $p$ the pilot is profitable using expected value.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Expected value is
		\[
		EV = 15\cdot p + (-5)\cdot(1-p)
		\]
		Expand and simplify.
		\[
		EV = 15p - 5 + 5p = 20p - 5
		\]
		Profitability condition is
		\[
		EV>0 \Rightarrow 20p-5>0
		\]
		Solve.
		\[
		20p>5 \Rightarrow p>\frac{1}{4}
		\]
		So the pilot is profitable when $p>\frac{1}{4}$.

The net profit from the upgrade depends on attendance conditions, where $S_1$ represents a high-attendance season and $S_2$ represents a low-attendance season. The values 21 and -9 are the museum's net profit in its budget units for the upgrade under $S_1$ and $S_2$.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lcc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p) & S_2\,(1-p) \\
			\hline
			\text{Upgrade} & 21 & -9 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p$ be the probability assigned by the museum to a high-attendance season $S_1$, so $1-p$ is the probability of a low-attendance season $S_2$ during the same season. Determine for which values of $p$ the upgrade is profitable.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Expected value is
		\[
		EV = 21\cdot p + (-9)\cdot(1-p)
		\]
		Expand and simplify.
		\[
		EV = 21p - 9 + 9p = 30p - 9
		\]
		Profitability condition is
		\[
		EV>0 \Rightarrow 30p-9>0
		\]
		Solve.
		\[
		30p>9 \Rightarrow p>\frac{3}{10}
		\]
		So the upgrade is profitable when $p>\frac{3}{10}$.

		\item
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A retailer must choose between two shipping options for the next quarter, and the net profit depends on the market condition. State $S_1$ represents a quarter with strong demand for expedited delivery, while $S_2$ represents a quarter with standard demand. The numbers in the table are the net profit in the retailer's accounting units for each option under the corresponding market condition.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lcc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p) & S_2\,(1-p) \\
			\hline
			A & 18 & 4 \\
			B & 12 & 9 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p$ be the retailer's probability that the strong-demand condition $S_1$ occurs in the quarter, so $1-p$ is the probability of $S_2$ in that quarter. When is option A better than option B using expected value.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Compute expected value of A.
		\[
		EV(A)=18\cdot p + 4\cdot(1-p)
		\]
		Expand and simplify.
		\[
		EV(A)=18p + 4 - 4p = 14p+4
		\]
		Compute expected value of B.
		\[
		EV(B)=12\cdot p + 9\cdot(1-p)
		\]
		Expand and simplify.
		\[
		EV(B)=12p + 9 - 9p = 3p+9
		\]
		A is better than B when
		\[
		EV(A)-EV(B)>0
		\]
		Substitute.
		\[
		(14p+4)-(3p+9)>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		11p-5>0
		\]
		So option A is better when $p>\frac{5}{11}$.

		\item
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A studio must choose among three publishing plans for a new release, and the net profit depends on the market response during the release window. State $S_1$ represents a strong market response and $S_2$ represents a weak market response in that window. The table entries are the studio's net profit in its reporting units for each plan under the corresponding market response.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lcc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p) & S_2\,(1-p) \\
			\hline
			A & 28 & 2 \\
			B & 18 & 10 \\
			C & 12 & 12 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p$ be the studio's probability of a strong market response $S_1$ during the release window, so $1-p$ is the probability of a weak response $S_2$ in the same window. Determine for which values of $p$ each plan is optimal.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Compute expected value of each plan.

		Plan A.
		\[
		EV(A)=28\cdot p + 2\cdot(1-p)=26p+2
		\]

		Plan B.
		\[
		EV(B)=18\cdot p + 10\cdot(1-p)
		\]
		Expand.
		\[
		EV(B)=18p + 10 - 10p = 8p+10
		\]

		Plan C.
		\[
		EV(C)=12\cdot p + 12\cdot(1-p)=12
		\]

		Now compare pairwise.

		A better than B.
		\[
		EV(A)-EV(B)>0 \Rightarrow (26p+2)-(8p+10)>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		18p-8>0 \Rightarrow p>\frac{4}{9}
		\]

		B better than C.
		\[
		EV(B)-EV(C)>0 \Rightarrow (8p+10)-12>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		8p-2>0 \Rightarrow p>\frac{1}{4}
		\]

		A better than C.
		\[
		EV(A)-EV(C)>0 \Rightarrow (26p+2)-12>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		26p-10>0 \Rightarrow p>\frac{5}{13}
		\]

		Determine the optimal plan by intervals.
		\begin{itemize}
			\item If $p<\frac{1}{4}$, then plan C is optimal.
			\item If $\frac{1}{4}<p<\frac{4}{9}$, then plan B is optimal.
			\item If $p>\frac{4}{9}$, then plan A is optimal.
		\end{itemize}

		\item
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A funding agency reviews two grant proposals for a single funding cycle, and the net benefit to the agency depends on three possible outcomes for that cycle. State $S_1$ represents a highly successful project outcome, $S_2$ represents a moderately successful outcome, and $S_3$ represents a low-impact outcome during the cycle. The values in the table are the net benefit in the agency's evaluation units for each proposal under each outcome.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lccc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p_1) & S_2\,(p_2) & S_3\,(1-p_1-p_2) \\
			\hline
			A & 16 & 8 & -4 \\
			B & 10 & 12 & 2 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p_1=P(S_1)$ be the probability of a highly successful outcome in the cycle, $p_2=P(S_2)$ be the probability of a moderately successful outcome in the same cycle, and $1-p_1-p_2=P(S_3)$ be the probability of a low-impact outcome. When is proposal A better than proposal B using expected value.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Compute expected value of A.
		\[
		EV(A)=16p_1 + 8p_2 + (-4)(1-p_1-p_2)
		\]
		Expand.
		\[
		EV(A)=16p_1 + 8p_2 -4 + 4p_1 + 4p_2 = 20p_1 + 12p_2 - 4
		\]
		Compute expected value of B.
		\[
		EV(B)=10p_1 + 12p_2 + 2(1-p_1-p_2)
		\]
		Expand.
		\[
		EV(B)=10p_1 + 12p_2 + 2 - 2p_1 - 2p_2 = 8p_1 + 10p_2 + 2
		\]
		A is better than B when
		\[
		EV(A)-EV(B)>0
		\]
		Substitute.
		\[
		(20p_1+12p_2-4)-(8p_1+10p_2+2)>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		12p_1+2p_2-6>0
		\]
		So proposal A is better when $6p_1+p_2-3>0$.

		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A service firm chooses between two staffing policies for the next operating year, and the net operating benefit depends on three workload conditions. State $S_1$ represents a year with heavy workload demand, $S_2$ represents a year with moderate workload demand, and $S_3$ represents a year with light workload demand. The numbers in the table are the net operating benefit in the firm's accounting units for each policy under each condition.

		\[
		\begin{array}{lccc}
			\hline
			& S_1\,(p_1) & S_2\,(p_2) & S_3\,(1-p_1-p_2) \\
			\hline
			A & 14 & 6 & -8 \\
			B & 8 & 10 & 1 \\
			\hline
		\end{array}
		\]

		Let $p_1=P(S_1)$ be the probability of heavy workload demand in the year, $p_2=P(S_2)$ be the probability of moderate workload demand in the same year, and $1-p_1-p_2=P(S_3)$ be the probability of light workload demand. When is policy A better than policy B.

		\subsection*{C7}
		Compute expected value of A.
		\[
		EV(A)=14p_1 + 6p_2 + (-8)(1-p_1-p_2)
		\]
		Expand.
		\[
		EV(A)=14p_1 + 6p_2 - 8 + 8p_1 + 8p_2 = 22p_1 + 14p_2 - 8
		\]
		Compute expected value of B.
		\[
		EV(B)=8p_1 + 10p_2 + 1(1-p_1-p_2)
		\]
		Expand.
		\[
		EV(B)=8p_1 + 10p_2 + 1 - p_1 - p_2 = 7p_1 + 9p_2 + 1
		\]
		A is better than B when
		\[
		EV(A)-EV(B)>0
		\]
		Substitute.
		\[
		(22p_1+14p_2-8)-(7p_1+9p_2+1)>0
		\]
		Simplify.
		\[
		15p_1+5p_2-9>0
		\]
		So policy A is better when $3p_1+p_2-\frac{9}{5}>0$.
