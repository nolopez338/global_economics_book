		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A student entrepreneur must decide whether to rent a single pop-up kiosk for one weekend.
		Market demand may be \textit{high} or \textit{low}. The estimated profit (in hundreds of dollars)
		is 36 if demand is high and 10 if demand is low.
		The estimated probabilities are: $P(\text{high demand})=0.60$ and $P(\text{low demand})=0.40$.
		
		\subsection*{Solution}
		\textbf{Step 1 --- Identify decision alternatives and states of nature.}
		Decision alternative: Rent one kiosk.
		States of nature: High demand and Low demand.
		
		\textbf{Step 2 --- Present the payoff table with probabilities included.}
		\begin{center}
			\textit{Payoff table}
			\begin{tabular}{l c c}
				\toprule
				State of nature & Probability & Rent kiosk \\
				\midrule
				High demand & 0.60 & 36 \\
				Low demand & 0.40 & 10 \\
				\bottomrule
			\end{tabular}
		\end{center}
		
		\textbf{Step 3 --- Apply the Expected Value formula.}
		\[
		EV(A_i)=\sum [\text{Payoff}\times\text{Probability}]
		\]
		For the kiosk decision:
		\[
		EV(\text{Rent kiosk})=(36\times0.60)+(10\times0.40)
		\]
		\[
		EV(\text{Rent kiosk})=21.6+4.0=25.6
		\]
		
		\textbf{Step 4 --- Compare expected values.}
		There is only one alternative, so its expected value is $25.6$.
		
		\textbf{Step 5 --- Final decision in business language.}
		Based on the expected value criterion, the entrepreneur should proceed with renting the kiosk because the project yields an expected profit of $25.6$ hundred dollars under the stated demand probabilities.
