		\subsection*{Problem 1}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A retail app company tracks the monthly spending on in-app subscriptions for all active users in one city.
		From full records, the population mean monthly spending is $\mu = 84$ USD and the population standard deviation is $\sigma = 18$ USD.
		A random sample of 45 users has sample mean $\bar{x} = 79$ USD and sample standard deviation $s = 16$ USD.
		Identify which values describe the population and which describe the sample, then interpret them in context.

		\subsection*{C1}
		The population parameters are the values computed from all active users in the city.
		So, the population mean is $\mu = 84$ USD and the population standard deviation is $\sigma = 18$ USD.
		These describe the center and spread of monthly subscription spending for the full user population.

		The sample statistics are the values computed from the 45 sampled users.
		So, the sample mean is $\bar{x} = 79$ USD and the sample standard deviation is $s = 16$ USD.
		These summarize the center and spread in the sample only.

		Because both spread measures are standard deviations, the units are USD for both.
		The sample mean is 5 USD below the population mean, and the sample spread is slightly smaller than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 2}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A bank studies monthly credit card balances of young professionals who opened cards in the last two years.
		Portfolio records report a population mean of $\mu = 1260$ USD and a population variance of $\sigma^2 = 144400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		A random sample of 60 account holders gives a sample mean of $\bar{x} = 1195$ USD and a sample variance of $s^2 = 129600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Classify population parameters and sample statistics, and interpret each quantity.

		\subsection*{C1}
		The population parameters are based on the entire portfolio.
		The population mean is $\mu = 1260$ USD and the population variance is $\sigma^2 = 144400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		These represent the average balance and long-run variability for all eligible account holders.

		The sample statistics are based on the 60 selected account holders.
		The sample mean is $\bar{x} = 1195$ USD and the sample variance is $s^2 = 129600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		These represent the average balance and variability in that sample.

		Both dispersion values are variances, so both are in $(\text{USD})^2$.
		The sample mean is lower than the population mean, and the sample variance is also lower than the population variance.

		\subsection*{Problem 3}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A freelance platform compares weekly gig income in two cities.
		Sample A is drawn from the population of drivers in City East, and Sample B is drawn from a different population in City West.
		For City East, the population mean is $\mu_A = 640$ USD, population variance is $\sigma_A^2 = 48400$ $(\text{USD})^2$, sample mean is $\bar{x}_A = 610$ USD, and sample variance is $s_A^2 = 40000$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		For City West, the population mean is $\mu_B = 720$ USD, population variance is $\sigma_B^2 = 57600$ $(\text{USD})^2$, sample mean is $\bar{x}_B = 755$ USD, and sample variance is $s_B^2 = 62500$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Identify population versus sample quantities for each city and interpret the comparison.

		\subsection*{C1}
		For City East, population parameters are $\mu_A = 640$ USD and $\sigma_A^2 = 48400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample statistics are $\bar{x}_A = 610$ USD and $s_A^2 = 40000$ $(\text{USD})^2$.

		For City West, population parameters are $\mu_B = 720$ USD and $\sigma_B^2 = 57600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample statistics are $\bar{x}_B = 755$ USD and $s_B^2 = 62500$ $(\text{USD})^2$.

		All means are in USD and all variances are in $(\text{USD})^2$.
		City East's sample mean is below its population mean, while City West's sample mean is above its population mean.
		City East's sample variance is below its population variance, while City West's sample variance is above its population variance.

		A housing analytics team studies monthly rent paid by first-job renters in one metro area.

		Sample 1 statistics are $\bar{x}_1 = 1385$ USD and $s_1 = 195$ USD.
		Sample 2 statistics are $\bar{x}_2 = 1450$ USD and $s_2 = 225$ USD.
		Each sample statistic describes only its own selected renters.

		All dispersion values are standard deviations, so the unit is USD.
		Sample 1 is below the population mean with slightly lower spread; Sample 2 is above the population mean with slightly higher spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 5}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A personal finance app tracks monthly savings deposits for users working side hustles.
		Three independent random samples are drawn from the same population of monthly deposits.
		Population values are $\mu = 520$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 32400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample 1 has $\bar{x}_1 = 500$ USD and $s_1^2 = 28900$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample 2 has $\bar{x}_2 = 545$ USD and $s_2^2 = 36100$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample 3 has $\bar{x}_3 = 515$ USD and $s_3^2 = 40000$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Classify all parameters and statistics and interpret their meaning.

		\subsection*{C1}
		The shared population parameters are the population mean $\mu = 520$ USD and population variance $\sigma^2 = 32400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		These describe the full user base of side-hustle savers.

		The sample statistics are:
		\[
		\bar{x}_1 = 500\text{ USD},\ s_1^2 = 28900\ (\text{USD})^2
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_2 = 545\text{ USD},\ s_2^2 = 36100\ (\text{USD})^2
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_3 = 515\text{ USD},\ s_3^2 = 40000\ (\text{USD})^2
		\]
		These summarize each sample separately.

		The first sample mean is below the population mean, the second is above, and the third is slightly below.
		Compared with the population variance, Sample 1 is less variable, while Samples 2 and 3 are more variable.

		\subsection*{Problem 6}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A travel budgeting site studies weekend trip spending by new graduates.
		For one random sample, a 95\% confidence interval for the sample mean spending is reported as $[410,\ 470]$ USD.
		From long-run market data, the population mean is $\mu = 450$ USD.
		Dispersion information is given in crossed formats: population variance $\sigma^2 = 6400$ $(\text{USD})^2$ and sample standard deviation $s = 90$ USD.
		Identify parameters and statistics, extract the sample mean from the interval, convert dispersion units when needed, and interpret.

		\subsection*{C1}
		The confidence interval provides endpoints for the sample mean estimate, so compute the midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x} = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{410+470}{2} = \frac{880}{2} = 440\text{ USD}
		\]
		So the sample mean statistic is $\bar{x} = 440$ USD.

		The population parameter for center is the population mean $\mu = 450$ USD.
		For dispersion, the population parameter is population variance $\sigma^2 = 6400$ $(\text{USD})^2$, while the sample statistic is sample standard deviation $s = 90$ USD.

		To compare spreads directly, convert population variance to population standard deviation:
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{\sigma^2} = \sqrt{6400\ (\text{USD})^2} = 80\text{ USD}
		\]
		Now both spreads are in USD: $\sigma = 80$ USD and $s = 90$ USD.
		The sample mean is 10 USD below the population mean, and the sample spread is larger than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 7}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		An e-commerce seller tracks daily net sales from a new product line.
		A 90\% confidence interval for the sample mean daily net sales is $[1180,\ 1320]$ USD.
		No population mean is provided.
		Dispersion is given as population standard deviation $\sigma = 140$ USD and sample variance $s^2 = 19600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Identify which values are parameters versus statistics, extract the sample mean, convert dispersion units for comparison, and interpret.

		\subsection*{C1}
		Extract the sample mean from the confidence interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x} = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{1180+1320}{2} = \frac{2500}{2} = 1250\text{ USD}
		\]
		So the sample mean statistic is $\bar{x} = 1250$ USD.

		For dispersion, the population parameter is $\sigma = 140$ USD and the sample statistic is $s^2 = 19600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Because these are in different forms, convert the sample variance to sample standard deviation:
		\[
		s = \sqrt{s^2} = \sqrt{19600\ (\text{USD})^2} = 140\text{ USD}
		\]
		Now both spreads are in USD and equal: $\sigma = 140$ USD and $s = 140$ USD.

		Since no population mean is given, only the sample center can be stated numerically as 1250 USD.
		The sample spread matches the population spread after conversion.

		\subsection*{Problem 8}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A fintech class compares monthly investing contributions in two different platforms.
		Sample A is from Platform Alpha users, and Sample B is from Platform Beta users, so they come from two different populations.
		For Platform Alpha, a 95\% confidence interval for the sample mean is $[260,\ 320]$ USD, the population mean is $\mu_A = 300$ USD, and population standard deviation is $\sigma_A = 70$ USD.
		For Platform Beta, a 90\% confidence interval for the sample mean is $[330,\ 390]$ USD, the population mean is $\mu_B = 360$ USD, and population variance is $\sigma_B^2 = 10000$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		The sample dispersion statistics are crossed: Sample A gives $s_A^2 = 5625$ $(\text{USD})^2$, and Sample B gives $s_B = 110$ USD.
		Extract each sample mean, classify parameters and statistics, convert where needed, and interpret.

		\subsection*{C1}
		For Platform Alpha, compute the sample mean midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_A = \frac{260+320}{2} = \frac{580}{2} = 290\text{ USD}
		\]
		Population parameters for Alpha are $\mu_A = 300$ USD and $\sigma_A = 70$ USD.
		Sample statistics for Alpha are $\bar{x}_A = 290$ USD and $s_A^2 = 5625$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Convert sample variance to sample standard deviation:
		\[
		s_A = \sqrt{5625\ (\text{USD})^2} = 75\text{ USD}
		\]

		For Platform Beta, compute the sample mean midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_B = \frac{330+390}{2} = \frac{720}{2} = 360\text{ USD}
		\]
		Population parameters for Beta are $\mu_B = 360$ USD and $\sigma_B^2 = 10000$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample statistics for Beta are $\bar{x}_B = 360$ USD and $s_B = 110$ USD.
		Convert population variance to population standard deviation:
		\[
		\sigma_B = \sqrt{10000\ (\text{USD})^2} = 100\text{ USD}
		\]

		Alpha's sample mean is below its population mean and has slightly larger spread than population.
		Beta's sample mean equals its population mean and has sample spread slightly above population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 9}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A streaming platform studies monthly entertainment spending by young renters in one city.
		Two random samples are drawn from the same population.
		Sample 1 has a 95\% confidence interval for the sample mean of $[205,\ 245]$ USD.
		Sample 2 has a 90\% confidence interval for the sample mean of $[220,\ 280]$ USD.
		The shared population mean is $\mu = 240$ USD and shared population standard deviation is $\sigma = 55$ USD.
		Sample dispersions are reported as $s_1^2 = 2500$ $(\text{USD})^2$ and $s_2^2 = 3600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Extract both sample means and interpret all quantities.

		\subsection*{C1}
		Compute each sample mean from its confidence-interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_1 = \frac{205+245}{2} = \frac{450}{2} = 225\text{ USD}
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_2 = \frac{220+280}{2} = \frac{500}{2} = 250\text{ USD}
		\]

		Because both samples come from one population, the population parameters are shared:
		$\mu = 240$ USD and $\sigma = 55$ USD.
		The sample statistics are $\bar{x}_1 = 225$ USD, $\bar{x}_2 = 250$ USD, $s_1^2 = 2500$ $(\text{USD})^2$, and $s_2^2 = 3600$ $(\text{USD})^2$.

		Convert sample variances to sample standard deviations for direct comparison with $\sigma$:
		\[
		s_1 = \sqrt{2500\ (\text{USD})^2} = 50\text{ USD}, \qquad
		s_2 = \sqrt{3600\ (\text{USD})^2} = 60\text{ USD}
		\]
		Sample 1 mean is below the population mean and Sample 2 mean is above it.
		Sample 1 spread is below population spread, while Sample 2 spread is above population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 10}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A payroll service studies weekly take-home pay for delivery workers in one metro area.
		Three independent random samples are drawn from the same population.
		The confidence intervals for sample means are:
		Sample 1 (95\%): $[680,\ 760]$ USD,
		Sample 2 (90\%): $[700,\ 780]$ USD,
		Sample 3 (95\%): $[740,\ 820]$ USD.
		The shared population mean is $\mu = 750$ USD and shared population variance is $\sigma^2 = 32400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		Sample dispersion statistics are crossed: $s_1 = 165$ USD, $s_2^2 = 28900$ $(\text{USD})^2$, and $s_3 = 190$ USD.
		Extract all sample means, classify parameters and statistics, convert where needed, and interpret.

		\subsection*{C1}
		Compute each sample mean as interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_1 = \frac{680+760}{2} = \frac{1440}{2} = 720\text{ USD}
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_2 = \frac{700+780}{2} = \frac{1480}{2} = 740\text{ USD}
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_3 = \frac{740+820}{2} = \frac{1560}{2} = 780\text{ USD}
		\]

		The population parameters shared by all three samples are population mean $\mu = 750$ USD and population variance $\sigma^2 = 32400$ $(\text{USD})^2$.
		The sample statistics are the three sample means above and dispersion values $s_1 = 165$ USD, $s_2^2 = 28900$ $(\text{USD})^2$, and $s_3 = 190$ USD.

		Convert the population variance to population standard deviation:
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{32400\ (\text{USD})^2} = 180\text{ USD}
		\]
		Convert Sample 2 variance to Sample 2 standard deviation:
		\[
		s_2 = \sqrt{28900\ (\text{USD})^2} = 170\text{ USD}
		\]
		Now all spreads are in USD: $\sigma = 180$ USD, $s_1 = 165$ USD, $s_2 = 170$ USD, and $s_3 = 190$ USD.
		Samples 1 and 2 have means below the population mean, Sample 3 is above it, and only Sample 3 has spread larger than the population spread.
