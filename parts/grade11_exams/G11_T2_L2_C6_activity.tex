		\item
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A transportation planner wants to estimate the mean commute time (in minutes) for a metropolitan rail line. Past studies show the population standard deviation is $\sigma = 8$ minutes. The planner wants a 99\% confidence estimate with a maximum error of $E = 2$ minutes. Determine the minimum sample size needed.
		
		\subsection*{C6}
		This solution sets the sample size so the margin of error does not exceed the target value by selecting $z^*$, substituting into the formula, and rounding up to the smallest integer that meets the requirement.
		
		Step 1: Identify the target error and confidence level. The maximum error is $E = 2$ minutes and the confidence level is 99\%, so $z^* = 2.576$.
		
		Step 2: Apply the sample size formula.
		\[
		n = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{2.576 \cdot 8}{2}\right)^2
		= \left(10.304\right)^2
		= 106.18
		\]
		
		Step 3: Round up and verify Criterion 6. Round up to $n = 107$. With $n=107$, the margin of error is at most 2 minutes, satisfying Criterion 6.

		\subsection*{C6}
		This solution determines the sample size that guarantees the margin of error does not exceed the specified value by using the $z^*$ value and rounding up.
		
		Step 1: Identify the target error and confidence level. The maximum error is $E = 3$ dollars and the confidence level is 95\%, so $z^* = 1.96$.
		
		Step 2: Apply the sample size formula.
		\[
		n = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{1.96 \cdot 15}{3}\right)^2
		= \left(9.80\right)^2
		= 96.04
		\]
		
		Step 3: Round up and verify Criterion 6. Round up to $n = 97$. With $n=97$, the margin of error is at most 3 dollars, satisfying Criterion 6.

		\subsection*{Problem description}
		An agriculture board wants to estimate the mean yield (in tons per hectare) of a crop. The population standard deviation is $\sigma = 4.5$ tons per hectare. The board wants a 90\% confidence estimate with a maximum error of $E = 1$ ton per hectare. Determine the minimum sample size required.
		
		\subsection*{C6}
		This solution identifies the target error and confidence level, applies the sample size formula, and rounds up to the smallest acceptable sample size.
		
		Step 1: Identify the target error and confidence level. The maximum error is $E = 1$ ton per hectare and the confidence level is 90\%, so $z^* = 1.645$.
		
		Step 2: Apply the sample size formula.
		\[
		n = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{1.645 \cdot 4.5}{1}\right)^2
		= \left(7.4025\right)^2
		= 54.80
		\]
		
		Step 3: Round up and verify Criterion 6. Round up to $n = 55$. With $n=55$, the margin of error is at most 1 ton per hectare, satisfying Criterion 6.
		
		\item
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A utilities department wants to estimate the mean monthly electricity bill (in dollars). The population standard deviation is $\sigma = 5.2$ dollars, the current sample mean is $\bar{x} = 120.5$ dollars, and $n_{\text{current}} = 49$ bills have been recorded. The department wants a 95\% confidence interval.
		
		(a) Construct the confidence interval for the mean bill.
		
		(b) How many additional observations are required to reduce the margin of error to $E = 1.0$ dollar?
		
		\subsection*{C5}
		This solution computes the confidence interval for the mean using the known standard deviation and the current sample information.
		
		Step 1: Compute the standard error.
		\[
		SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n_{\text{current}}}}
		= \frac{5.2}{\sqrt{49}}
		= 0.74
		\]
		
		Step 2: Construct the confidence interval. For 95\% confidence, $z^* = 1.96$.
		\[
		\bar{x} \pm z^* SE = 120.5 \pm 1.96(0.74)
		= 120.5 \pm 1.46
		= (119.04,\ 121.96)
		\]
		
		Conclusion. The 95\% confidence interval is $(119.04,\ 121.96)$ dollars.
		
		\subsection*{C6}
		This subsection determines the required sample size so the margin of error does not exceed the target amount.
		
		Step 1: Use the target error and confidence level. The target margin of error is $E = 1.0$ dollar and for 95\% confidence, $z^* = 1.96$.
		
		Step 2: Compute the required sample size.
		\[
		n_{\text{required}} = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{1.96 \cdot 5.2}{1.0}\right)^2
		= \left(10.192\right)^2
		\approx 103.88
		\]
		
		Step 3: Determine additional observations. Round up to $n_{\text{required}} = 104$. The additional observations needed are $104 - 49 = 55$.
		
		Conclusion. The department needs 55 more observations so the error will not exceed 1.0 dollar.

		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A school district is estimating the mean final exam score for a course. The population standard deviation is $\sigma = 6$ points, the current sample mean is $\bar{x} = 78.2$ points, and $n_{\text{current}} = 25$ scores are available. The district wants a 90\% confidence interval.
		
		(a) Construct the confidence interval for the mean score.
		
		(b) How many additional observations are required to reduce the margin of error to $E = 1.2$ points?
		
		\subsection*{C5}
		This solution calculates the confidence interval for the mean using the current sample information and known standard deviation.
		
		Step 1: Compute the standard error.
		\[
		SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n_{\text{current}}}}
		= \frac{6}{\sqrt{25}}
		= 1.2
		\]
		
		Step 2: Construct the confidence interval. For 90\% confidence, $z^* = 1.645$.
		\[
		\bar{x} \pm z^* SE = 78.2 \pm 1.645(1.2)
		= 78.2 \pm 1.97
		= (76.23,\ 80.17)
		\]
		
		Conclusion. The 90\% confidence interval is $(76.23,\ 80.17)$ points.
		
		\subsection*{C6}
		This subsection determines the required sample size so the margin of error does not exceed the target amount.
		
		Step 1: Use the target error and confidence level. The target margin of error is $E = 1.2$ points and for 90\% confidence, $z^* = 1.645$.
		
		Step 2: Compute the required sample size.
		\[
		n_{\text{required}} = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{1.645 \cdot 6}{1.2}\right)^2
		= \left(8.225\right)^2
		\approx 67.65
		\]
		
		Step 3: Determine additional observations. Round up to $n_{\text{required}} = 68$. The additional observations needed are $68 - 25 = 43$.
		
		Conclusion. The district needs 43 more observations so the error will not exceed 1.2 points.

The population standard deviation is $\sigma = 18$ liters, the current sample mean is $\bar{x} = 210$ liters, and $n_{\text{current}} = 64$ households have been surveyed. The authority wants a 99\% confidence interval.
		
		(a) Construct the confidence interval for the mean daily water use.
		
		(b) How many additional observations are required to reduce the margin of error to $E = 4$ liters?
		
		\subsection*{C5}
		This solution computes the confidence interval for the mean using the known standard deviation and the current sample.
		
		Step 1: Compute the standard error.
		\[
		SE = \frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n_{\text{current}}}}
		= \frac{18}{\sqrt{64}}
		= 2.25
		\]
		
		Step 2: Construct the confidence interval. For 99\% confidence, $z^* = 2.576$.
		\[
		\bar{x} \pm z^* SE = 210 \pm 2.576(2.25)
		= 210 \pm 5.80
		= (204.20,\ 215.80)
		\]
		
		Conclusion. The 99\% confidence interval is $(204.20,\ 215.80)$ liters.
		
		\subsection*{C6}
		This subsection determines the required sample size so the margin of error does not exceed the target amount.
		
		Step 1: Use the target error and confidence level. The target margin of error is $E = 4$ liters and for 99\% confidence, $z^* = 2.576$.
		
		Step 2: Compute the required sample size.
		\[
		n_{\text{required}} = \left(\frac{z^*\,\sigma}{E}\right)^2
		= \left(\frac{2.576 \cdot 18}{4}\right)^2
		= \left(11.592\right)^2
		\approx 134.36
		\]
		
		Step 3: Determine additional observations. Round up to $n_{\text{required}} = 135$. The additional observations needed are $135 - 64 = 71$.
		
		Conclusion. The authority needs 71 more observations so the error will not exceed 4 liters.
