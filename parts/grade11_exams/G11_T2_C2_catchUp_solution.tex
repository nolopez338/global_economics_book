		\subsection*{Problem 1}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A rental platform tracks monthly studio rent for young professionals in one city area.
		The population mean rent is $\mu = 1850$ USD, and one recent sample has mean $\bar{x} = 1810$ USD.
		The population variance is $\sigma^2 = 10000$ (USD)$^2$, and the sample variance is $s^2 = 8100$ (USD)$^2$.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 1850$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 10000$ (USD)$^2$.
		These describe the long-run center and long-run spread of all monthly studio rents in this city area.

		The sample statistic for location is $\bar{x} = 1810$ USD.
		This is the sample mean rent from the observed group, not the full population.

		For spread, the sample statistic is $s^2 = 8100$ (USD)$^2$.
		Convert both variances to standard deviations for an easier spread comparison:
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{10000} = 100 \text{ USD},
		\qquad
		s = \sqrt{8100} = 90 \text{ USD}.
		\]
		So the population standard deviation/spread is 100 USD, and the sample standard deviation/spread is 90 USD.

		The sample mean is 40 USD below the population mean, and the sample spread is slightly smaller than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 2}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A company reviews first-year salary offers for recent graduates in a data support role.
		The population mean offer is $\mu = 52000$ USD, and a sample of new offers has mean $\bar{x} = 53100$ USD.
		The population standard deviation is $\sigma = 6000$ USD, and the sample standard deviation is $s = 5500$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 52000$ USD and $\sigma = 6000$ USD.
		The population mean gives the long-run average offer, and the population standard deviation/spread gives typical distance from that average across all offers.

		The sample statistics are $\bar{x} = 53100$ USD and $s = 5500$ USD.
		These summarize only the observed sample of offers.

		To express spread in variance form:
		\[
		\sigma^2 = 6000^2 = 36{,}000{,}000\ \text{(USD)}^2,
		\qquad
		s^2 = 5500^2 = 30{,}250{,}000\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]
		So the population variance is $36{,}000{,}000$ (USD)$^2$ and the sample variance is $30{,}250{,}000$ (USD)$^2$.

		The sample mean is higher than the population mean, while the sample spread is somewhat narrower than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 3}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		Two gig platforms are analyzed separately.
		Platform A (food delivery) has population mean weekly income $\mu_A = 720$ USD and sample mean $\bar{x}_A = 750$ USD.
		Its population variance is $\sigma_A^2 = 4900$ (USD)$^2$, and sample variance is $s_A^2 = 6400$ (USD)$^2$.
		Platform B (freelance design) has population mean weekly income $\mu_B = 980$ USD and sample mean $\bar{x}_B = 940$ USD.
		Its population standard deviation is $\sigma_B = 80$ USD, and sample standard deviation is $s_B = 70$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		For Platform A, the population parameters are $\mu_A = 720$ USD and $\sigma_A^2 = 4900$ (USD)$^2$.
		The sample statistics are $\bar{x}_A = 750$ USD and $s_A^2 = 6400$ (USD)$^2$.
		Convert variance to standard deviation:
		\[
		\sigma_A = \sqrt{4900} = 70\ \text{USD},
		\qquad
		s_A = \sqrt{6400} = 80\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		For Platform B, the population parameters are $\mu_B = 980$ USD and $\sigma_B = 80$ USD.
		The sample statistics are $\bar{x}_B = 940$ USD and $s_B = 70$ USD.
		In variance form:
		\[
		\sigma_B^2 = 80^2 = 6400\ \text{(USD)}^2,
		\qquad
		s_B^2 = 70^2 = 4900\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		For Platform A, the sample mean is above the population mean and the sample spread is larger; for Platform B, the sample mean is below the population mean and the sample spread is smaller.

		\subsection*{Problem 4}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A bank studies monthly credit card spending of customers in their 20s from one metro area.
		Both samples come from the same population.
		Population values are $\mu = 1350$ USD and $\sigma = 300$ USD.
		Sample 1 has $\bar{x}_1 = 1290$ USD and $s_1 = 280$ USD.
		Sample 2 has $\bar{x}_2 = 1410$ USD and $s_2 = 320$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters for this metro area are $\mu = 1350$ USD and $\sigma = 300$ USD.
		These describe the long-run average spending and long-run standard deviation/spread for all customers in their 20s in this target population.

		The sample means are $\bar{x}_1 = 1290$ USD and $\bar{x}_2 = 1410$ USD.
		These are sample statistics, each computed from one sample, so they can differ from each other and from $\mu$.

		The sample spreads are $s_1 = 280$ USD and $s_2 = 320$ USD, also sample statistics.
		In variance form:
		\[
		\sigma^2 = 300^2 = 90000\ \text{(USD)}^2,
		\quad
		s_1^2 = 280^2 = 78400\ \text{(USD)}^2,
		\quad
		s_2^2 = 320^2 = 102400\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		Sample 1 is below the population mean with smaller spread, while Sample 2 is above the population mean with larger spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 5}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A savings app tracks monthly deposits toward emergency funds from users in their 20s.
		All three samples come from the same population.
		Population mean and variance are $\mu = 420$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 1600$ (USD)$^2$.
		Sample A: $\bar{x}_A = 400$ USD, $s_A^2 = 900$ (USD)$^2$.
		Sample B: $\bar{x}_B = 435$ USD, $s_B^2 = 2500$ (USD)$^2$.
		Sample C: $\bar{x}_C = 415$ USD, $s_C^2 = 3600$ (USD)$^2$.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 420$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 1600$ (USD)$^2$.
		So the population standard deviation/spread is
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{1600} = 40\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		The sample means are $\bar{x}_A = 400$ USD, $\bar{x}_B = 435$ USD, and $\bar{x}_C = 415$ USD.
		These are sample statistics describing each observed group.

		The sample variances are $s_A^2 = 900$, $s_B^2 = 2500$, and $s_C^2 = 3600$ (USD)$^2$.
		Convert to sample standard deviations/spreads:
		\[
		s_A = \sqrt{900} = 30\ \text{USD},
		\qquad
		s_B = \sqrt{2500} = 50\ \text{USD},
		\qquad
		s_C = \sqrt{3600} = 60\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		Compared with the population, Sample A has a lower mean and smaller spread, Sample B has a higher mean and larger spread, and Sample C has a slightly lower mean with larger spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 6}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A streaming platform studies monthly subscription spending by young adults.
		A 95\% confidence interval for one sample mean is $[58, 74]$ USD.
		The population variance is $\sigma^2 = 400$ (USD)$^2$, and the sample standard deviation is $s = 24$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameter provided for spread is the population variance $\sigma^2 = 400$ (USD)$^2$.
		This corresponds to population standard deviation/spread
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{400} = 20\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		No population mean is provided in this problem.

		From the confidence interval endpoints, the sample mean is the midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x} = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{58+74}{2} = \frac{132}{2} = 66\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		The sample spread is given as $s = 24$ USD, which is a sample statistic.
		In variance form:
		\[
		s^2 = 24^2 = 576\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		The sample mean is 66 USD, and the sample spread (24 USD) is higher than the population spread (20 USD).

		\subsection*{Problem 7}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A travel-budget app tracks weekend trip spending for users in their 20s.
		For one sample, the 90\% confidence interval for the sample mean is $[420, 480]$ USD.
		The population mean is $\mu = 460$ USD, the population standard deviation is $\sigma = 70$ USD, and the sample standard deviation is $s = 60$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 460$ USD and $\sigma = 70$ USD.
		They represent the long-run average trip budget and long-run population standard deviation/spread.

		Compute the sample mean from the interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x} = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{420+480}{2} = \frac{900}{2} = 450\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		So $\bar{x} = 450$ USD is the sample mean statistic.

		The sample spread is $s = 60$ USD.
		In variance form for comparison:
		\[
		\sigma^2 = 70^2 = 4900\ \text{(USD)}^2,
		\qquad
		s^2 = 60^2 = 3600\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		The sample mean is 10 USD below the population mean, and the sample spread is smaller than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 8}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		Two small online stores are analyzed with separate populations.
		Store A (handmade accessories) has a 95\% confidence interval for one sample mean daily revenue of $[240, 300]$ USD.
		Its population mean is $\mu_A = 275$ USD, population standard deviation is $\sigma_A = 50$ USD, and sample variance is $s_A^2 = 1600$ (USD)$^2$.
		Store B (digital templates) has a 90\% confidence interval for one sample mean daily revenue of $[330, 390]$ USD.
		Its population mean is $\mu_B = 350$ USD, population variance is $\sigma_B^2 = 3600$ (USD)$^2$, and sample standard deviation is $s_B = 55$ USD.

		\subsection*{C2}
		For Store A, population parameters are $\mu_A = 275$ USD and $\sigma_A = 50$ USD.
		Find the sample mean from the interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_A = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{240+300}{2} = \frac{540}{2} = 270\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		The sample spread is given as variance $s_A^2 = 1600$ (USD)$^2$, so
		\[
		s_A = \sqrt{1600} = 40\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		For Store B, population parameters are $\mu_B = 350$ USD and $\sigma_B^2 = 3600$ (USD)$^2$.
		Its population standard deviation/spread is
		\[
		\sigma_B = \sqrt{3600} = 60\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		Find the sample mean from the interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_B = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{330+390}{2} = \frac{720}{2} = 360\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		The sample spread is $s_B = 55$ USD, so
		\[
		s_B^2 = 55^2 = 3025\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		Store A has a sample mean slightly below its population mean with smaller spread, while Store B has a sample mean above its population mean with slightly smaller spread than its population.

		\subsection*{Problem 9}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		A delivery company studies weekly earnings for riders in one city, so both samples come from the same population.
		Sample 1 has a 95\% confidence interval for the sample mean: $[680, 760]$ USD.
		Sample 2 has a 90\% confidence interval for the sample mean: $[700, 780]$ USD.
		Population values are $\mu = 740$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 6400$ (USD)$^2$.
		Sample spread data are crossed: $s_1 = 70$ USD and $s_2^2 = 4900$ (USD)$^2$.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 740$ USD and $\sigma^2 = 6400$ (USD)$^2$.
		So the population standard deviation/spread is
		\[
		\sigma = \sqrt{6400} = 80\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		Compute each sample mean from interval midpoints:
		\[
		\bar{x}_1 = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{680+760}{2} = \frac{1440}{2} = 720\ \text{USD},
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_2 = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{700+780}{2} = \frac{1480}{2} = 740\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		For spread, Sample 1 gives $s_1 = 70$ USD, so
		\[
		s_1^2 = 70^2 = 4900\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]
		Sample 2 gives $s_2^2 = 4900$ (USD)$^2$, so
		\[
		s_2 = \sqrt{4900} = 70\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		Sample 1 mean is below the population mean, Sample 2 mean matches the population mean, and both sample spreads are smaller than the population spread.

		\subsection*{Problem 10}
		\subsection*{Problem description}
		An investment app tracks monthly net deposits (deposits minus withdrawals) for users in their 20s.
		All three samples come from the same population.
		Population values are $\mu = 620$ USD and $\sigma = 90$ USD.
		The confidence intervals for sample means are:
		Sample A (95\%): $[580, 640]$ USD,
		Sample B (90\%): $[600, 660]$ USD,
		Sample C (95\%): $[550, 630]$ USD.
		Sample spread values are $s_A^2 = 6400$ (USD)$^2$, $s_B = 85$ USD, and $s_C^2 = 10000$ (USD)$^2$.

		\subsection*{C2}
		The population parameters are $\mu = 620$ USD and $\sigma = 90$ USD.
		In variance form,
		\[
		\sigma^2 = 90^2 = 8100\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]

		Compute each sample mean from the confidence interval midpoint:
		\[
		\bar{x}_A = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{580+640}{2} = \frac{1220}{2} = 610\ \text{USD},
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_B = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{600+660}{2} = \frac{1260}{2} = 630\ \text{USD},
		\]
		\[
		\bar{x}_C = \frac{L+U}{2} = \frac{550+630}{2} = \frac{1180}{2} = 590\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		For spread, Sample A gives variance, so
		\[
		s_A = \sqrt{6400} = 80\ \text{USD}.
		\]
		Sample B gives standard deviation, so
		\[
		s_B^2 = 85^2 = 7225\ \text{(USD)}^2.
		\]
		Sample C gives variance, so
		\[
		s_C = \sqrt{10000} = 100\ \text{USD}.
		\]

		Sample B is above the population mean, while Samples A and C are below it; spreads are smaller than population for A and B but larger for C.
