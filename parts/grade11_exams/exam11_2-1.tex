\subsection{Assessment and evaluation}\label{sec:grade11-exam11-2-1-evaluation}

This exam evaluates only the criteria C1--C5. Each criterion is evaluated across the three problems below.
A criterion is passed if it is correctly activated in at least two of the three problems.
The overall exam result is binary (Passed/Not passed).

\subsection{Problem 1}\label{sec:grade11-exam11-2-1-problem1}

A city budgeting office wants to estimate the average weekly transit subsidy per rider for two pilot zones.
Zone A recorded the following sample of weekly subsidy amounts (USD):

\begin{itemize}
	\item Zone A sample (\(n_A = 6\)): 18, 22, 19, 21, 20, 17.
	\item Zone B sample (\(n_B = 8\)): 24, 26, 23, 25, 27, 22, 24, 26.
\end{itemize}

An annual audit provides the population standard deviations for the zones: \(\sigma_A = 3.5\) and \(\sigma_B = 4.0\).
For classroom purposes, suppose the true population mean weekly subsidy is \(\mu_A = 20\) dollars in Zone A and \(\mu_B = 25\) dollars in Zone B.
These true means cannot be obtained or known in the real budgeting context and are provided only as academic reference values.
They are included so students can compare the sample means \(\bar{x}_A\) and \(\bar{x}_B\) to the population means in C2 and to motivate why point and interval estimation are needed in C3 when \(\mu\) is unknown in practice.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(C\arabic*)}, leftmargin=2.2em]
	\item Compute the sample mean and sample standard deviation for each zone.
	\item Distinguish between the sample statistics and the population parameters given in the description.
	\item Explain why the office should use interval estimation instead of a single point estimate for budgeting.
	\item Interpret the meaning of a 95\% confidence interval for the mean weekly subsidy in each zone.
	\item Construct 95\% confidence intervals for the mean weekly subsidy in Zone A and Zone B using the known population standard deviations.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Problem 2}\label{sec:grade11-exam11-2-1-problem2}

A regional warehouse studies the number of orders processed per hour to plan staffing.
A random sample of 60 hours is summarized in grouped form below. The operations team also reports a known population standard deviation of \(\sigma = 6.2\) orders per hour.
For academic purposes only, assume the true population mean order rate is \(\mu = 22\) orders per hour.
This true mean cannot be obtained or known in the real warehouse setting and is provided solely as a reference value.
It is included so students can compare the sample mean \(\bar{x}\) to the population mean in C2 and to highlight in C3 why point and interval estimation are needed when \(\mu\) is unknown in practice.

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabularx}{0.6\linewidth}{@{}L{0.35\linewidth}C@{}}
		\toprule
		Orders per hour (\(x\)) & Frequency (\(f\)) \\
		\midrule
		14 & 8 \\
		18 & 16 \\
		22 & 20 \\
		26 & 10 \\
		30 & 6 \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(C\arabic*)}, leftmargin=2.2em]
	\item Compute the grouped sample mean and sample standard deviation of orders per hour.
	\item Identify which values represent sample statistics and which represent population parameters.
	\item Explain the difference between reporting a point estimate and reporting a confidence interval in this setting.
	\item Interpret a 95\% confidence interval for the mean hourly order rate in terms of staffing decisions.
	\item Construct a 95\% confidence interval for the mean hourly order rate using the known population standard deviation.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Problem 3}\label{sec:grade11-exam11-2-1-problem3}

A logistics firm monitors the time (in minutes) required to load containers at a port.
A sample of 80 loading times is grouped into class intervals below. The firm has a historical estimate of the population standard deviation, \(\sigma = 5.8\) minutes.
For classroom reference, suppose the true population mean loading time is \(\mu = 32\) minutes.
This true mean cannot be obtained or known in the real port operation and is provided only for academic comparison.
It is included so students can compare the sample mean \(\bar{x}\) to the population mean in C2 and to motivate in C3 why point and interval estimation are required when \(\mu\) is unknown in practice.

\begin{center}
	\begin{tabularx}{0.75\linewidth}{@{}L{0.45\linewidth}C@{}}
		\toprule
		Loading time interval (minutes) & Frequency (\(f\)) \\
		\midrule
		20--24 & 12 \\
		25--29 & 18 \\
		30--34 & 20 \\
		35--39 & 17 \\
		40--44 & 13 \\
		\bottomrule
	\end{tabularx}
\end{center}

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{(C\arabic*)}, leftmargin=2.2em]
	\item Use class midpoints to compute the grouped sample mean and sample standard deviation.
	\item Distinguish the sample statistics from the population parameter supplied in the description.
	\item Explain why an interval estimate is more informative than a single mean when negotiating service-level targets.
	\item Interpret a 95\% confidence interval for the mean loading time in the context of port scheduling.
	\item Construct a 95\% confidence interval for the mean loading time using the known population standard deviation.
\end{enumerate}
